## Observed visual language

The reference is a **watercolour and cut-paper storybook collage**. Painted figures and scenery retain uneven outlines, pigment pooling and paper grain. White cutout rims and small cast shadows make separate pieces legible. It is an illustrated paper theatre, with flat layers arranged at different apparent depths rather than photoreal 3D.

The recurring base is warm ivory paper and ochre/brown corkboard or craft paper. Orange/coral, mustard yellow, muted teal and olive provide accents. A saturated blue starfield and an underwater chapter temporarily expand that palette. Ordinary handmade materials — paper, a box, scissors, adhesive tape and pictures — make abstract ideas tangible.

Typography is not one uniform treatment. The opening uses individual cut-magazine letter tiles: slightly rotated blocks, mixed letterforms and black/cream/mustard/coral/blue colours. A later document uses small conventional black serif type on squared paper. Memory captions sit on little light-coloured paper labels. Keep title collage letters as an accent and preserve readable product branding at the end.

## Adaptation to the Converse story

Preserve the already created Nano Banana 2 family, places and shoes. Give those paintings a coherent paper-archive treatment: ivory paper borders, restrained shadows, tape corners, a small date stamp and warm stock. Use the 18 Nano subjects as a source library rather than displaying all three views of a continuity sheet as if they were a scene.

A useful original organising idea is **“the shoe is the family archive”**. The laces become a drawn route between years; the shoe silhouette, sole and patch repeatedly anchor transitions. The attic box opens into a theatre of memories. Return to the same shoe in the present, now understood differently.

Suggested graphic actions, all original implementation choices:

1. Start with a quiet attic card and a small handwritten request from Mom. Reveal Luna and the box through a paper window.
2. Bring the old photo forward as a physical postcard, then expand it to become the basketball scene. Match the position of the black shoe through the change.
3. Make a bouncing ball or circular eyelet carry a brief circular reveal to the salsa floor. A red shoe joins the black shoe.
4. Stack wedding, baby and gift moments as individual cards. Keep each emotional pair together rather than using a wall of rapid fragments.
5. Let the archive fill the shape of a Converse sole, shoe or star emblem rather than copying the reference's heart collage.
6. Finish with Luna, Mom and the inherited pair. The thought resolves to **“CONVERSE. CONSERVE WHAT MATTERS.”** A simple, readable end card should have more stillness than the memory montage.

Motion should have contrast: quick, confident paper entrances followed by readable holds; a few small rotations and restrained overshoot; quiet moments for faces and the exchange between generations. Avoid moving every element at once. The reference’s tactile warmth is more useful here than a barrage of transitions.

## Sound direction for our film (proposal, not reference analysis)

Create our own bed: soft paper/fabric rustles in the attic, a basketball bounce that becomes a percussion beat, a warmer salsa colour for the couple, then a lighter musical space for the gift and return. Keep Mom’s call legible. Avoid using the inspiration video's audio or implying it was analysed.
